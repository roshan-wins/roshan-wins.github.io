NACA 2412 with Gurney flaps and serrations

A standard NACA 2412 airfoil tested in CFD with attachments such as Gurney flaps and serrated trailing edges, to see what each one does and where it is worth using.

Method
CFD
Results
Confidential

The question

Small passive attachments can change what a wing does. A Gurney flap is a short tab at the trailing edge, square to the pressure side, usually a percent or two of the chord tall; it turns the flow at the trailing edge and adds lift for a drag cost. Serrated trailing edges break up the trailing-edge flow in a different way. The study put both on a standard NACA 2412 and compared them in CFD to see what each one does and where it is worth using.

Results

The set-up and the results are confidential, so this page shows the question and the baseline flow only.

ABOUT THE PICTURE

The streamlines come from a constant-strength vortex panel method with 280 panels and a Kutta condition at the trailing edge. Its lift coefficient, 0.74 at 4°, is in line with thin-airfoil theory with a thickness correction. Viscous effects, and so the attachments, are outside what it can show.
