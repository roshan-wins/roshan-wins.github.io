UAV wing optimization

Parametric external-aerodynamics CFD on UAV wing geometries, with NSGA-II multi-objective optimization to pick the aerodynamic attachments that balance drag against efficiency, and a NACA 2412 airfoil tested with Gurney flaps and serrated edges.

Role
CFD engineer intern
Where
Reude Technologies
When
January–May 2024
Tools
Ansys Fluent, NSGA-II

The problem

A UAV wing has to carry the aircraft with as little drag as possible, and attachments on the wing shift that balance. The question was which attachment configuration gives the best trade-off between drag and aerodynamic efficiency.

Approach

  • Parametric external-aerodynamics CFD on the wing geometries, so each design in the space could be generated and simulated the same way.
  • NSGA-II, a genetic algorithm for several objectives, searched the attachment design space for designs on the Pareto front between drag and efficiency, and the final configuration was picked from that front.

Result

The final configuration was chosen from the Pareto front. Its geometry and numbers are confidential, so the chart above shows the method, not the data.

NACA 2412 with Gurney flaps and serrations

Alongside the wing work, I tested a standard NACA 2412 airfoil in CFD with attachments such as Gurney flaps and serrated trailing edges, to see what each one does and where it is worth using. A Gurney flap is a short tab at the trailing edge, square to the pressure side, usually a percent or two of the chord tall; it turns the flow at the trailing edge and adds lift for a drag cost. Serrated trailing edges break up the trailing-edge flow in a different way.

The set-up and the results are confidential too, so the picture shows the baseline airfoil only.

ABOUT THE AIRFOIL PICTURE

The streamlines come from a constant-strength vortex panel method with 280 panels and a Kutta condition at the trailing edge. Its lift coefficient, 0.74 at 4°, is in line with thin-airfoil theory with a thickness correction. Viscous effects, and so the attachments, are outside what it can show.
