What the model is for

In a steer-by-wire truck there is no steering column: a road-wheel actuator turns the wheels on command. To design its controller you need a plant model that keeps what governs how torque and angle travel from the motor to the wheels, and nothing else. Two of us are responsible for that mechanical model, as part 1 of the Vehicle Dynamics Project Course at KTH.

The chain, reduced

  • Motor: an ideal torque source, saturated at ±20 N·m. No rotor inertia, back EMF or electrical lag yet.
  • Worm gear: an ideal 20 : 1 reduction, with friction Tf1 taken off the motor torque before the reduction.
  • Recirculating-ball gear: two inertias, Ja on the gear side and Jb for the ball nut and pitman arm, coupled by a torsional spring and damper. Friction Tf2 acts on Jb only, the mass whose speed drives it.
  • Steering gear and pitman arm: another ideal 20 : 1 reduction, so 400 : 1 overall and 8000 N·m at the pitman arm in the ideal case.
  • Drag link and wheels: a third compliant element and one equivalent inertia, loaded by the road torque.

Every inertia, stiffness and damping value is expressed on the shaft where it acts; the pitman-arm inertia enters Jb as Jpm/is².

Two equations that do a lot of work

Without friction and load, the ball-gear pair is a rigid body plus one oscillation:

ωn = √( kc (Ja + Jb) / (Ja Jb) ),   ζ = (cc / 2) √( (Ja + Jb) / (kc Ja Jb) )

Both contacts use the same smooth friction law, Coulomb plus a load-dependent loss plus viscous drag, with a tanh so it always opposes motion:

Tf = [ TC + (1 − η) |Tthru| ] tanh(ω / ωε) + b ω

Checks so far

In the recorded run, the ball-nut friction Tf2 stayed at about 0.6 to 0.7 N·m while the shaft turned at up to about 3 rad/s. It fell to about zero as the speed approached zero and reversed sign with the speed, as it should.

Known limits

  • The ideal motor makes high-frequency bandwidth look better than it is.
  • No backlash, end stops or tooth elasticity in the gears.
  • No stiction or Stribeck effect, so the mechanism can creep under a small load.
  • The wheel angle is an equivalent angle; its mapping to the real wheel angle and the truck's parameters are still open.
