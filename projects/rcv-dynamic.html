The vehicle

RCV-Dynamic is a four-wheel research vehicle at KTH with a 700 N·m motor inside each wheel. Steering, camber, drive and brake torque and the active suspension are all controlled separately at each corner, which leaves little room for the parts that hold everything together.

What I did

  • Led the detail design and parametric 3D CAD of the subframe, the A-arms and the aluminium suspension parts, and owned the release of their design documentation and manufacturing specifications.
  • Made the manufacturing and assembly drawings with full GD&T. Datum schemes and tolerances were set against each supplier's process capability, to cut lead time.
  • Found and onboarded suppliers with wire EDM, then changed part geometry to suit that process.
  • Selected and packaged off-the-shelf hardware (linear actuators, pedals, radiators) and routed its wiring so harnesses cleared the assembly paths and were protected for rough use.
  • Built and assembled the prototype hardware in the lab, took junior engineers through build and fit-up, and inspected parts against the drawings.

Designing for the machines that make it

A part is only finished when someone can make it. I found new manufacturers for the parts and adapted the existing designs to the machines they had, above all wire-cut EDM: the part geometry changed to suit the process, and the datums and tolerances on each drawing were set against what that supplier's process could hold.
